\section{The story in 60 seconds}
\begin{say}
A ring-oscillator VCO changes frequency with temperature, so a PLL built around it has to work harder, or drop out of lock range, as the chip heats up. We built a complete integer-N charge-pump PLL in the open sky130 process and simulated it in ngspice. We measured the drift at all five process corners and found a 12.5$\times$ spread in temperature coefficient, not the 3.4$\times$ we reported in Review 1. We then designed a correction voltage that adds $k_1(T-27)+k_2(T-27)^2$ to the VCO control voltage. At the typical corner, the second-order version cuts the drift span from 8.51\% to 1.12\%. It meets our $\pm$0.3\% target only at the ff and sf corners. We validated the result at held-out temperatures and with a 30-sample Monte Carlo analysis using paired statistics. We also fixed the cold-start problem left open in Review 1: the loop now locks from a true 0 V start in 1352 ns. Auditing the loop afterwards, we found it was underdamped ($\zeta=0.33$), retuned the loop-filter resistor from 1.7 to 3.6 k$\Omega$, and verified by transistor-level simulation that $\zeta$ is now about 0.70 and the loop locks inside the 2 $\mu$s spec at all five corners. The price is a slower cold start (839 ns with the original filter).
\end{say}

\noindent\textbf{The three things Review 2 added}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Characterisation:} the full 5-corner $\times$ 6-temperature VCO grid.
\item \textbf{Compensation:} 1st- and 2nd-order correction, fitted per corner and validated honestly.
\item \textbf{Robustness:} the cold-start fix, plus a Monte Carlo analysis with correct statistics.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The system: what each block does}
\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{pll_block_diagram.png}\end{center}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Reference:} 28.275 MHz. The target output is $8\times$ that, $\approx$226 MHz.
\item \textbf{PFD (phase-frequency detector):} compares the reference edge against the divided VCO edge and outputs UP or DN pulses in proportion to the phase error. It is built from edge detectors and NOR SR-latches rather than resettable D flip-flops, because a reliable asynchronous reset fights the latch's own feedback.
\item \textbf{Charge pump:} converts UP/DN pulses into current pulses of $I_{cp}=20\,\mu$A into the loop filter. In Review 2 this is an idealised behavioural source; the transistor-level version is Review 3.
\item \textbf{Loop filter:} $R_2=3.6\,$k$\Omega$ (retuned; the original design used 1.7 k$\Omega$) in series with $C_1=68\,$pF, and $C_2=6.8\,$pF in parallel. It integrates the current into a control voltage $V_{ctrl}$. $R_2$ adds the zero that keeps the loop stable; $C_2$ smooths the ripple.
\item \textbf{VCO:} a 5-stage current-starved ring. $V_{ctrl}$ sets the starving current, which sets the delay per stage and therefore the frequency. $K_{VCO}\approx890$ MHz/V at 0.9 V (tt, local slope). There is no hard oscillation floor: below about 0.65 V it is under 18 MHz, and at 0.5 V it is sub-MHz.
\item \textbf{Divider $\div8$:} a chain of toggle flip-flops. It feeds the divided clock back to the PFD.
\end{itemize}

\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=0.5\linewidth]{vco_schematic.png}\hfill\includegraphics[width=0.48\linewidth]{tuning.png}\end{center}

\noindent\textbf{How to read the tuning curve.} The slope of this curve is $K_{VCO}$, and it is not constant: the curve is S-shaped, 18 MHz at 0.65 V to 358 MHz at 1.40 V (tt). At 0.9 V the local slope is $\approx$890 MHz/V at tt. Below about 0.65 V the ring does not stop; it drops into subthreshold operation and the frequency falls by roughly $2.5$--$3\times$ for every $-50$ mV (41.3, 18.0, 6.47, 2.09, 0.63 MHz at 0.70, 0.65, 0.60, 0.55, 0.50 V). So there is \emph{no hard floor}, but at a 0 V start the divided clock is effectively absent, which is why the cold start was a problem.

\section{Result 1: uncompensated drift across all corners}
\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=0.62\linewidth]{drift_uncomp.png}\hfill\includegraphics[width=0.36\linewidth]{tempco_bar.png}\end{center}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Procedure:} fix $V_{ctrl}=0.9$ V, sweep $T\in\{-40,-20,0,27,50,85\}$ \textdegree C, and do this at all 5 corners (30 simulations).
\item \textbf{Tempco} $=(f_{85}-f_{-40})/(f_{27}\cdot125)$ in ppm/\textdegree C. Values: sf 112, ff 332, tt 681, ss 1070, fs 1399.
\item \textbf{The correction to Review 1:} with only 3 corners we reported a 3.4$\times$ spread. The full grid shows 12.5$\times$, because fs, the worst corner, had never been simulated.
\item \textbf{Insight:} the skew corners bracket the range: sf (fast-NMOS / slow-PMOS) is lowest and fs (slow-NMOS / fast-PMOS) is highest. The tempco ranks with the \emph{NMOS drive} at 0.9 V, as the table shows.
\end{itemize}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}\toprule
Corner & sf & ff & tt & ss & fs\\\midrule
NMOS $I_d$ at $|V_{gs}|=0.9$ V, 27 \textdegree C ($\mu$A) & 45.1 & 42.8 & 36.2 & 30.3 & 28.2\\
Tempco (ppm/\textdegree C) & 112 & 332 & 681 & 1070 & 1399\\
$f$ at 0.9 V, 27 \textdegree C (MHz) & 246.2 & 279.2 & 226.2 & 178.0 & 183.7\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{careful}
\textbf{Corner labels.} In the sky130B PDK, \texttt{sf} is NMOS \emph{fast} (threshold $-27$ mV, mobility $+5\%$) with PMOS slow, and \texttt{fs} is NMOS \emph{slow} ($+27$ mV, $-5\%$) with PMOS fast. Say ``sf (fast-NMOS / slow-PMOS)'' and ``fs (slow-NMOS / fast-PMOS)''; never ``sf = slow N / fast P''. The skew corners move the NMOS threshold by $\pm27$ mV against only $\pm15$ mV for ss/ff, so they are more extreme on the NMOS than ss/ff, which is why they bracket the range.
\end{careful}
\begin{say}
\textbf{Why frequency rises with temperature (supported interpretation).} $V_{ctrl}$ drives the NMOS starving gate directly and sets the PMOS bias through the Mnb$\to$Mpb mirror, so the NMOS overdrive sets the starving current. At 0.9 V that overdrive is small. Rising temperature lowers $V_{th}$ and lowers mobility; with little overdrive, the $V_{th}$ drop is a large fraction of it and beats the mobility loss, so current and frequency \emph{rise}. Less overdrive gives a stronger positive tempco, which is exactly the ranking above (sf 112 $<$ ff 332 $<$ tt 681 $<$ ss 1070 $<$ fs 1399). Check: at tt with $V_{ctrl}=1.4$ V (more overdrive) the tempco is \emph{negative}, $-816$ ppm/\textdegree C ($f$ = 380.2, 358.1, 343.7 MHz at $-40$, 27, 85 \textdegree C). So the tempco changes sign between 0.9 V and 1.4 V, i.e.\ a zero-tempco bias point exists. This is the textbook picture, and the sign flip plus the ordering support it. It is still an \emph{interpretation}: we did not run a separate isolation experiment.
\end{say}

\section{The compensation network}
\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{compensation_arch.png}\end{center}
\[ V_{ctrl,eff} = V_{ctrl,nom} + k_1(T-27) + k_2(T-27)^2 \]
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Implementation:} an ideal behavioural voltage source (an ngspice B-source) that reads the simulator temperature. \textbf{It is not transistors yet.} In silicon, this role is played by a PTAT/CTAT current generator.
\item \textbf{$k_1$ (analytical):} from the slope of the fitted $f(T)$ divided by $K_{VCO}$ (the analytic value $k_1=-2.163\times10^{-4}$ V/\textdegree C used 669 MHz/V). Because the frequency rises with $T$, $k_1$ is negative.
\item \textbf{$k_2$, first attempt (naive):} derived from the curvature of the fit. It \emph{over-corrected} the cold end (+4.13\% at $-40$ \textdegree C, and a 4.49\% span, worse than 1st order's 3.05\%). There were two causes: it assumed $K_{VCO}$ is constant, which it is not, and it used the wrong $K_{VCO}$ (669 instead of the true local $\approx$887 MHz/V), which overestimated the needed correction by about $1.3\times$.
\item \textbf{$k_2$, refined:} refit against the \emph{measured} residual left after 1st-order compensation. This step needed no extra simulation. At tt: $k_1=-1.320\times10^{-4}$ V/\textdegree C and $k_2=2.891\times10^{-7}$ V/\textdegree C$^2$.
\item \textbf{Per corner:} $k_1$ and $k_2$ are refit separately at every corner. In silicon this corresponds to a per-corner trim.
\end{itemize}

\section{Monte Carlo and the statistics}
\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=0.42\linewidth]{mc_paired.png}\hfill\includegraphics[width=0.54\linewidth]{mc_means.png}\end{center}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Setup:} tt corner with sky130's mismatch models switched on (\texttt{mc\_mm\_switch=1}). Each \emph{seed} is one virtual chip with random transistor mismatch.
\item \textbf{Metric:} $|$drift$|$ $= |f_{85}-f_{-40}|$ in MHz.
\item \textbf{Paired design:} the \emph{same} seed runs through both the 1st- and 2nd-order networks, so chip-to-chip variation cancels out of the comparison.
\item \textbf{Results:} mean $|$drift$|$ is 18.93 MHz uncompensated (n=12), 5.85 MHz for 1st order (n=30) and 3.37 MHz for 2nd order (n=30).
\item \textbf{2nd vs 1st:} a reduction of 2.48 MHz, 95\% CI [0.81, 4.15]. The paired t-test and the Wilcoxon signed-rank test are both significant, and 2nd order wins in 23 of 30 seeds.
\item \textbf{Two tests:} the t-test assumes normality; Wilcoxon does not. Both agreeing makes the result robust. Both are hand-implemented in \texttt{scripts/stats.py} (there is no scipy in this environment) and self-tested.
\end{itemize}
\begin{careful}
\textbf{The correction we made ourselves (own it, it is a strength).} The first analysis compared \emph{signed} drift. First order over-corrects (its drift is negative, $-5.7$ MHz) while second order under-corrects slightly ($+2.3$ MHz). Comparing signed values therefore made the difference look large simply because the sign flipped. The right quantity is $|$drift$|$. On $|$drift$|$ at n=12 the difference was \emph{not} significant, so we raised n to 30, and that is what earns the claim. This is recorded in \texttt{docs/stats\_methodology.md} and in the project charter's decision log.
\end{careful}

\section{Closed loop and the cold-start fix}
\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=0.66\linewidth]{acq_boost.png}\end{center}
\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=0.7\linewidth]{lock.png}\end{center}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The problem:} at 0 V the VCO is essentially off. There is no hard floor, but below about 0.65 V it runs under 18 MHz (under 8\% of the 226 MHz target) and at 0.5 V below 1 MHz, so the divided clock is effectively absent. The PFD sees only reference edges, and pumping up at the nominal 20 $\mu$A is too slow.
\item \textbf{The fix:} the charge pump runs at $5\times I_{cp}$ until the divider produces its first edge. An edge detector feeding a one-shot latch (an integrating capacitor charged by a B-source, not a digital latch with an unknown initial state) then drops it back to 20 $\mu$A for normal, stable tracking.
\item \textbf{Result with the retuned loop filter ($R_2=3.6$ k$\Omega$):} lock in \textbf{1352 ns} from a true 0 V start, and \textbf{503 ns} from a coarse-tuned 0.85 V start (tt, 27 \textdegree C). Both are inside the $<$2 $\mu$s spec.
\item \textbf{Original design ($R_2=1.7$ k$\Omega$), kept as the ``before'':} 839 ns from 0 V and 577 ns from 0.85 V. (Review 1's ``450 ns analytical prediction'' matched only by coincidence; see the loop-dynamics subsection.)
\item \textbf{Lock definition:} $V_{ctrl}$ enters and stays within $\pm2\%$ of its final 0.9 V.
\item \textbf{The cold start is slower after the retune (839 $\to$ 1352 ns, +61\%).} We do not hide this: it is the price of the better damping. Cold-start peak $V_{ctrl}$ is 1.001 V (1.008 V originally). Do not claim that the retune made everything faster; only the coarse-start lock is faster.
\item \textbf{The ringing around 600--800 ns on the original cold-start curve} is the loop capturing phase after the fast pump-up. It settled inside the spec, but the original loop was genuinely underdamped (next subsection). We have not separately attributed it to the boost switching off, so don't claim that.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Loop dynamics, honestly: found, fixed, verified}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Found:} with the original $R_2=1.7$ k$\Omega$, $C_1=68$ pF, $I_{cp}=20\,\mu$A, $N=8$ and the measured $K_{VCO}=887$ MHz/V, the natural frequency is $f_n\approx0.91$ MHz and the damping is $\zeta\approx0.33$. Phase margin is about 30--33\textdegree\ (the $100\zeta$ rule; $C_2$ erodes it a little more). The loop was \textbf{underdamped}: from a 0.85 V start $V_{ctrl}$ overshot to 0.93 V, and the cold start rang.
\item \textbf{What Review 1 got wrong:} it put $K_{VCO}$ in rad/s/V into $\omega_n^2=I_{cp}K_{VCO}/(NC_1)$ and \emph{dropped the $1/2\pi$} of the PFD/charge-pump gain ($I_{cp}/2\pi$ per radian). The correct form is $\omega_n^2=I_{cp}K_{VCO,rad}/(2\pi NC_1)$ and $\zeta=R_2C_1\omega_n/2$. Review 1 therefore claimed $f_n=1.98$ MHz, $\zeta=0.72$, 70--72\textdegree\ margin, 2.85 MHz bandwidth and a 450 ns lock; with the same wrong 669 MHz/V and the right formula one gets $f_n=0.79$ MHz and $\zeta=0.29$.
\item \textbf{Fixed:} $\zeta$ depends only on $R_2$ at fixed $f_n$, so we raised $R_2$ from 1.7 to \textbf{3.6 k$\Omega$}. $f_n$ stays about 0.91 MHz, $\zeta$ goes from 0.33 to about \textbf{0.70}, and the phase margin from about 30\textdegree\ to about \textbf{65--70\textdegree} (100$\zeta$ rule). Nothing else in the loop changed ($I_{cp}$, $C_1$, $C_2$, $N$, $f_{ref}$).
\item \textbf{Verified at transistor level (tt, coarse start):} ripple 8.6 $\to$ 0.35 mV p-p, final frequency error 0.78\% $\to$ 0.008\%, peak $V_{ctrl}$ 0.930 $\to$ 0.922 V, lock 577 $\to$ 503 ns. The independent averaged model (\texttt{scripts/}\allowbreak\texttt{loop\_check.py}, driven by the measured tuning curve) gave 573 ns against 577 ns for the original loop, so it was a trustworthy check of the measured lock times; the 450 ns ``prediction'' matched only by coincidence. Without the $5\times$ boost, the averaged model from 0 V does not settle (the pump saturates, overshoot 1.79 V), consistent with the cold-start problem.
\item \textbf{The trade-off:} the 0 V cold start is slower (839 $\to$ 1352 ns). Both are within the 2 $\mu$s spec.
\end{itemize}
\begin{careful}
\textbf{Limits of the retune claim.} Only $R_2=1.7$, 3.0 and 3.6 k$\Omega$ were simulated at transistor level (plus an averaged-model sweep of 1.7--6 k$\Omega$). We do not claim 3.6 k$\Omega$ is optimal, and values above 3.6 k$\Omega$ were not simulated at transistor level. The 503 ns figure is set by a small overshoot to 0.922 V that sits just outside the $\pm2\%$ band (0.918 V) and decays into it; the averaged model has a slightly smaller overshoot (0.914 V) and enters the band at about 110 ns. The models agree on the dynamics, but the $\pm2\%$ band metric is sensitive at the band edge, so quote 503 ns as a transistor-level number, not as a precise property of the loop. The 0 V cold start was simulated at tt only.
\end{careful}

\section{Methodology and research framing}
\begin{center}\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{method_flow.png}\end{center}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Hypothesis H1 (the research question):} the error in a compensation network's residual grows as $\varepsilon_{res}\approx C\cdot\varepsilon_{raw}$. In words, the better you compensate, the more a small error in the drift \emph{measurement} (for example, from the open PDK's models versus a commercial one) is magnified in what remains. Testing it needs a second tool flow (Cadence Virtuoso/Spectre), which is \textbf{pending lab access}. The strength ($\alpha$) sweep we ran provides a range of $C$ values ready for that test; on its own it is \emph{not} the H1 test.
\item \textbf{Novelty (be precise):} US Patent 9,748,962 B2 (Infineon, 2017) already covers generic quadratic temperature compensation. We do \emph{not} claim quadratic compensation as new. Our claim is narrower: a quantified, statistically validated, per-corner study of it for a \emph{ring-VCO PLL, closed-loop, on an open PDK}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Corrections made after Review 2}\label{sec:corr}
\begin{owned}
After the slides were made we re-checked the work and corrected five things and fixed one of them; say so openly. \textbf{(1)} The sf/fs labels were backwards: sf is fast-NMOS / slow-PMOS and fs is slow-NMOS / fast-PMOS. \textbf{(2)} The tempco explanation (``mismatched corners balance the current sources'') was wrong; the tempco follows NMOS overdrive, and the sign flips at higher $V_{ctrl}$ (tt, 1.4 V: $-816$ ppm/\textdegree C). \textbf{(3)} $K_{VCO}$ was 669 MHz/V from a quadratic fit over the whole curve; the local value at 0.9 V is $\approx$890 MHz/V (887), which also explains part of the analytic $k_2$ over-correction. \textbf{(4)} Review 1's loop design dropped a $1/2\pi$, so the original loop ($R_2=1.7$ k$\Omega$) was underdamped ($\zeta\approx0.33$, $\sim$30\textdegree\ margin, not 0.72 and 70\textdegree), though its measured lock times were confirmed by an independent model (573 vs 577 ns). \emph{This is now fixed and verified:} $R_2$ was retuned to 3.6 k$\Omega$ ($\zeta\approx0.70$, PM $\sim$65--70\textdegree) and re-simulated at transistor level: ripple 8.6 $\to$ 0.35 mV, frequency error 0.78\% $\to$ 0.008\%, coarse lock 577 $\to$ 503 ns, cold-start lock 839 $\to$ 1352 ns (slower, within spec), and lock inside 2 $\mu$s at all five corners (ss slowest, 967 ns). \textbf{(5)} The ``0.63 V oscillation floor'' was an artefact of a short simulation window; the ring keeps oscillating at low frequency. \textbf{Unchanged:} spans, tempcos (112/332/681/1070/1399), the 2nd-order results, held-out checks, Monte Carlo statistics and the open-loop results are direct simulation results; the compensation and Monte Carlo use the free-running VCO with an ideal control voltage, so the loop retune does not touch them.
\end{owned}

\section{Likely questions and answers}
\qa{Your spec is $\pm$0.3\% and you only meet it at 2 of 5 corners. Isn't that a failure?}{It is a gap, and we say so on the slide. The correction is currently a fixed curve per corner. Closing the gap at tt/ss/fs needs a transistor-level network whose PTAT slope itself tracks process, and/or a trim. That is Review 3 work. What Review 2 establishes is how large the problem is, and that 2nd order beats 1st order with statistical confidence.}

\qa{The compensation is an ideal source. How real is this?}{It is a behavioural model, as is standard at this stage: it tells us \emph{what} correction voltage is needed. In silicon it would be a PTAT/CTAT current generator driving a resistor; a quadratic term can come from combining PTAT and a squared term (for example, a translinear stage). Building that at transistor level is Review 3.}

\qa{Why a ring VCO and not an LC oscillator?}{A ring is small, has a wide tuning range and needs no inductor, which suits a digital-friendly open PDK. Its disadvantage is exactly what we study: large temperature sensitivity (and worse phase noise).}

\qa{Why is there no phase noise or jitter result?}{Out of scope for Review 2, which focuses on temperature. A transient-noise or PSS-style analysis in ngspice is limited; this would be part of the Spectre arm.}

\qa{Why sky130 and ngspice?}{They are fully open, so the work is reproducible by anyone; the PDK is pinned by commit via volare. The trade-off is less mature models, which is exactly why H1 plans a Spectre cross-check.}

\qa{How do you know you didn't overfit? Six points, two coefficients.}{Four held-out temperatures that were never used in fitting, at every corner, all fall inside the fitted envelope. The Monte Carlo analysis also tests the same coefficients on 30 mismatched chips.}

\qa{Why was the naive $k_2$ wrong?}{It assumed $K_{VCO}$ is constant with temperature and voltage. It is not: the tuning curve is nonlinear. It also used 669 MHz/V instead of the true local $\approx$887, which overestimated the correction about $1.3\times$. Refitting against the measured 1st-order residual absorbs both.}

\qa{Why is Monte Carlo only at tt, and why is the baseline n=12?}{Compute time. The baseline effect is huge (12/12 seeds, reduction CI [9.98, 15.57] MHz), so n=12 is already decisive there. The 1st-vs-2nd difference is small, so it needed n=30.}

\qa{What was the statistics error?}{We compared signed drift, but 1st order over-corrects and 2nd order under-corrects slightly, so the sign flip inflated the difference. We switched to $|$drift$|$, found it not significant at n=12, and re-ran at n=30, where it is significant. It is documented in the repo.}

\qa{What is new, given the Infineon patent?}{Quadratic compensation itself is not new. What is new is the quantified, per-corner, statistically validated study on a ring-VCO PLL in closed loop on an open PDK, and the H1 error-amplification question.}

\qa{Why does the frequency increase with temperature?}{At 0.9 V the NMOS starving transistor has little overdrive, so the fall of $V_{th}$ with temperature beats the fall of mobility and the current rises. At higher $V_{ctrl}$ the tempco flips sign: tt at 1.4 V is $-816$ ppm/\textdegree C, so a zero-tempco bias point exists. The sign flip and the corner ordering support this; it is still an interpretation, because we did not run a separate isolation experiment.}

\qa{Is your loop stable? What is your phase margin?}{Yes, and now well damped. With the measured $K_{VCO}=887$ MHz/V and the retuned $R_2=3.6$ k$\Omega$: $f_n\approx0.91$ MHz, $\zeta\approx0.70$, phase margin about 65--70\textdegree\ (the $100\zeta$ rule, an estimate, not a measured Bode plot). The original $R_2=1.7$ k$\Omega$ gave $\zeta\approx0.33$ and about 30\textdegree: stable but underdamped. Review 1 claimed 0.72 and 70\textdegree\ for that filter only because it dropped the $1/2\pi$ in the charge-pump gain. We found that, retuned, and verified it by transistor-level simulation.}

\qa{Why did you retune $R_2$, and what did it cost?}{Because the corrected formulas ($\omega_n^2=I_{cp}K_{VCO,rad}/(2\pi NC_1)$, $\zeta=R_2C_1\omega_n/2$) showed the original 1.7 k$\Omega$ gave $\zeta=0.33$. Raising it to 3.6 k$\Omega$ keeps $f_n\approx0.91$ MHz and gives $\zeta\approx0.70$. Verified at transistor level (tt, 0.85 V start): ripple 8.6 $\to$ 0.35 mV p-p, final frequency error 0.78\% $\to$ 0.008\%, lock 577 $\to$ 503 ns. The cost: the true 0 V cold start is slower, 839 $\to$ 1352 ns (+61\%). Both are inside the 2 $\mu$s spec. We simulated only 1.7, 3.0 and 3.6 k$\Omega$, so we do not claim 3.6 is optimal.}

\qa{Does it lock at every corner?}{Yes, the retuned loop does, from a 0.85 V start with the nominal charge pump: ff 241 ns, sf 419, tt 503, fs 576 and ss 967 ns, all inside 2 $\mu$s. ss is slowest because it needs $V_{ctrl}\approx0.968$ V and has the weakest NMOS. The ss figure is from a 5 $\mu$s run (in the 2 $\mu$s run it is still settling at 224.56 MHz). Honest caveat: the original loop had not finished settling at ff/sf/fs/ss within its 2 $\mu$s run (0.45\% low, 1.3\% high, 1.2\% high and 3.6\% low at 2 $\mu$s), so we give no old lock times there, except ss at 1737 ns from the 5 $\mu$s run. The 0 V cold start was simulated only at tt.}

\qa{Why does sf have a lower tempco than ff? Isn't ff the fastest?}{sf is fast-NMOS / slow-PMOS, and the NMOS drive sets the starving current. sf has the most NMOS drive (45.1 $\mu$A at 0.9 V against 42.8 for ff), so the threshold-versus-mobility trade is closest to its zero-tempco point. The skew corners shift NMOS $V_{th}$ by $\pm27$ mV against $\pm15$ mV for ss/ff, so they are the extremes. Note ff is the fastest in frequency (279 MHz); sf is only the fastest in NMOS current.}

\qa{How did you get $K_{VCO}$?}{As the local slope at 0.9 V: a central difference at $\pm50$ mV from a fine sweep, 887 MHz/V at tt (about 890). The earlier 669 came from a quadratic fit across the whole S-shaped 0.65--1.4 V curve and was wrong; we found and corrected it. Per corner it is 580 / 940 / 890 / 820 / 1020 for sf / ff / tt / ss / fs.}

\qa{Does the VCO really stop at 0.63 V?}{No. That was an artefact of a short simulation window. With long windows the ring keeps oscillating in subthreshold: 41.3, 18.0, 6.47, 2.09 and 0.63 MHz at 0.70, 0.65, 0.60, 0.55 and 0.50 V. The correct statement is that there is no hard floor, but below about 0.65 V the VCO is under 8\% of the target, so from a 0 V start the divided clock is effectively absent. The cold-start fix is unchanged: the PFD sees only reference edges and the nominal 20 $\mu$A pump is too slow.}

\qa{What does the acquisition boost do to stability?}{It is only active before the first divider edge, while the loop is still open. After that, the charge pump runs at the nominal 20 $\mu$A for which the loop was designed, (and for which $\zeta\approx0.70$ with the retuned $R_2$), so stability during tracking is unaffected by the boost. The retune does change the cold start itself: from 0 V it now takes 1352 ns instead of 839 ns.}

\qa{Why a latch-based PFD?}{A resettable-D-flip-flop PFD needs a clean asynchronous reset. In our hand-built cells, that reset fought the latch's bistable feedback. Edge detectors plus NOR SR-latches were reliable, and the PFD was verified standalone in Review 1.}

\qa{What is left for Review 3?}{A transistor-level charge pump and compensation network; layout in Magic with DRC and LVS (netgen); post-layout simulation; and the Spectre arm with the H1 regression. The deadline is 28.10.2026.}

\section{Glossary}
\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=1pt]
\item[Process corner] The five sets of device models: tt (typical), ff/ss (both fast/slow), sf/fs (sf: NMOS fast + PMOS slow; fs: NMOS slow + PMOS fast).
\item[Tempco] The fractional frequency change per \textdegree C, in ppm/\textdegree C.
\item[Span] Max $-$ min frequency over temperature, as a percentage of the 27 \textdegree C frequency.
\item[Compensation ratio $C$] Baseline span divided by compensated span. Bigger is better.
\item[PTAT / CTAT] Proportional / complementary to absolute temperature: bias sources that rise or fall linearly with $T$.
\item[$K_{VCO}$] The VCO gain, $df/dV_{ctrl}$, quoted as the local slope at 0.9 V (about 890 MHz/V at tt).
\item[Damping ratio $\zeta$ / phase margin] How well damped the loop is; $\zeta=R_2C_1\omega_n/2$. Phase margin is about $100\zeta$ degrees. Original loop: 0.33, $\sim$30\textdegree; retuned: 0.70, $\sim$65--70\textdegree.
\item[Lock time] Time until $V_{ctrl}$ stays within $\pm2\%$ of its final value.
\item[Seed] One Monte Carlo sample, i.e.\ one random mismatch instance (a virtual chip).
\item[Paired t-test / Wilcoxon] Tests on per-seed differences. The t-test assumes normality; Wilcoxon uses ranks and does not.
\item[95\% CI] The range that contains the true mean difference with 95\% confidence. If it excludes 0, the effect is significant.
\item[Held-out] Data points kept out of the fit and used only to test it.
\end{description}
